# Draft response to Chris and Ram

**Subject:** TCST target-clarity decay sweep and half-domain sanity check

Hi Chris and Ram,

I followed up on the concern about why the adaptive strategy did not initially
show a lower ground-truth clarity deficit in the half-domain experiment. I ran
two related checks:

1. an oracle transect restricted to the interesting half of the domain; and
2. a sweep of the target-clarity decay rate
   `lambda_cd = {0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}` in both the static
   half-domain and moving-pocket environments.

For each decay rate I reran all eight half-domain strategies and all seven
moving-pocket strategies with the same initial condition, environment, mission
duration, and measurement-noise seed.

## What the sanity check shows

The restricted half-domain transect behaves as expected. Relative to the full
transect, it:

- increases the fraction of measurements collected in the interesting region
  from 43.19% to 95.68%; and
- reduces deficit against the fixed ground-truth target from 0.09339 to
  0.07680, a 17.8% reduction.

Its global wind-field RMSE is worse (3.423 versus 1.552), which is also expected:
the vehicle deliberately gives up reconstruction of the uninteresting half.
This confirms that the ground-truth deficit metric does reward spatial coverage
of the complete interesting region.

## Why the adaptive strategy did not automatically improve deficit

The animations clarify the discrepancy. With a selective target map, the
adaptive controller reaches an interesting area but often remains within a
small portion of that half. It therefore collects many relevant measurements
without distributing those measurements across the entire high-target-clarity
region. The result is:

- a high percentage of near-rated measurements;
- poor global reconstruction outside the sampled area; and
- residual clarity deficit across unsampled portions of the interesting half.

In contrast, the oracle half-domain transect systematically covers the entire
interesting region. The original result was therefore not caused by the metric
failing to reward interesting-region coverage. It exposed a difference between
**finding an interesting region** and **covering the complete interesting
region**.

## Effect of the target-clarity decay rate

For adaptive ergodic control in the half-domain case:

| lambda_cd | Global RMSE | Own-target deficit | Fixed-target deficit | In-target measurements |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1.671 | 0.16525 | 0.08619 | 46.66% |
| 0.025 | 2.101 | 0.08669 | 0.08669 | 91.96% |
| 0.05 | 2.535 | 0.09317 | 0.09317 | 92.84% |
| 0.1 | 2.686 | 0.09590 | 0.09590 | 95.21% |
| 0.25 | 2.839 | 0.09425 | 0.09425 | 95.57% |

The `lambda_cd = 0` adaptive-ergodic run is numerically identical to the
non-adaptive ergodic run in both environments. This confirms the proposed
limiting case: under a spatially constant target, adaptive ergodic control
reduces to the non-adaptive strategy.

A small positive value changes the behavior substantially. At 0.025, targeted
sampling rises by 45.30 percentage points while global RMSE rises by 25.7%.
Increasing the rate from 0.025 to 0.25 adds only 3.61 percentage points of
targeted sampling, while RMSE increases by another 35.2%. The fixed-target
deficit also becomes worse rather than better. This is consistent with the
animations: sharper target maps increasingly favor exploitation of a localized
estimated region instead of coverage of the full interesting half.

The moving-pocket case shows that the response is not monotonic. Adaptive
ergodic control reaches its highest in-target percentage at `lambda_cd = 0.1`
(79.53%), but falls to 32.36% at 0.25. A very sharp map is more sensitive to
estimation error: if the estimated pocket is displaced, the controller can
strongly exploit the wrong location.

## Deficit definitions

The attached plot reports two clarity deficits:

- **Own-target deficit:** each run is evaluated against the target generated by
  its own `lambda_cd`. This measures whether the strategy met the requirement
  it was given.
- **Fixed-target deficit:** every run is evaluated against the same
  ground-truth target generated with `lambda_cd = 0.25`. This permits a direct
  comparison of trajectories across the sweep.

The distinction matters because increasing `lambda_cd` lowers the requested
clarity over most of the domain. Own-target deficit can therefore decrease
because the target has become narrower, even if the trajectory has not
improved. The non-adaptive and transect trajectories demonstrate this: their
paths, RMSE, and measurement distributions are unchanged across the sweep, but
their own-target deficit changes with the evaluation target. Their fixed-target
deficit remains constant.

## Current interpretation

My current interpretation is that `lambda_cd` controls an explicit
exploration--exploitation tradeoff:

- zero produces broad non-adaptive ergodic coverage;
- a small positive value efficiently biases sampling toward interesting
  regions;
- a larger value can over-concentrate the trajectory and leave substantial
  portions of the desired region uncovered; and
- in dynamic environments, excessive selectivity can amplify target-estimation
  error.

For these deterministic runs, 0.025 is the clearest balanced value across the
two environments, while 0.1 is preferable if moving-target acquisition is the
primary objective. I do not think the current evidence supports presenting
0.25 as universally optimal.

One implementation detail remains before using every BB-IPP result in the same
limiting-case argument: adaptive and non-adaptive BB-IPP currently optimize
different reward maps (target clarity versus clarity deficit). Consequently,
their `lambda_cd = 0` runs are not equivalent. The ergodic comparison does not
have this issue and passes the zero-decay check exactly.

These are matched single-seed sensitivity runs, so I would treat the observed
optima as mechanistic evidence rather than statistically final values. If this
narrative and metric treatment look right, my next step would be to incorporate
the sanity check, decay-rate sensitivity, and coverage explanation into the
TCST paper and reviewer response, then determine whether a smaller replicated
seed study is needed for the key rates.
